Alex van Someren and Graeme Blance Join Cloudberry VC as Venture Partners

One built the BBC Micro, co-founded nCipher and ran Amadeus Capital. The other put cameras in phones, first at STMicroelectronics and then at Apple. Here is what they, and all our Venture Partners, bring to founders.

Two more Venture Partners join the Cloudberry VC Jam! Acorn urchin and ARM collaborator Alex van Someren, and former Apple camera and optics lead Graeme Blance.

Alex van Someren

Alex started his career at Acorn Computers in the 1980s, where he helped develop the BBC Micro and the Acorn Electron. He went on to co-found several tech companies, including nCipher and ANT Software, which both listed on the London Stock Exchange and were later acquired. He then spent 11 years as Managing Partner at Amadeus Capital, investing in early-stage deep tech across the UK and Europe. Today he is Executive Chair of the quantum computing company Photonic.

He has also been the UK's Chief Scientific Adviser for National Security and a consultant to the €1B NATO Innovation Fund. He is a Fellow of the Royal Academy of Engineering and an Honorary Professor at the University of Manchester.

Graeme Blance

Graeme comes to us with over 30 years of experience pushing technology boundaries, from startups to high-volume multinationals.

He started at a Scottish startup that pitched the idea that one day we'd all carry cameras in our mobile phones, and then made it a reality by industrialising CMOS imaging and camera module manufacturing within STMicroelectronics.

In 2011, Graeme was headhunted by Apple's camera team, where he played a lead role in driving camera component technologies and architecture from the iPhone 4S through the iPhone 18 Pro. More recently, he has been working on vision and imaging products that remain in the "surprise and delight" pipeline.

He's now back in Europe and looking forward to bringing his experience to the startup scene.

What our Venture Partners actually do

Scroll through any VC website and you'll find them. Venture Partners. Impressive names and nice headshots. What they actually do is less clear.

Ours are deeply involved in the investment process and in portfolio support. They bring in deals from networks built over decades in hardware, semiconductors, photonics and deep tech in general. They join due diligence and ask the questions you only learn to ask after sitting on the operational side of the table. When a company comes to our Investment Committee, they sit in the room and advise.

Then the real work starts.

Every deep tech company hits the same moment. The prototype works in the lab. Now someone has to build ten thousand of them, at the right cost, with a yield that doesn't eat the margin. That is when experience counts. IP strategy. Scaling the organisation, sites and manufacturing. A resilient global supply chain. Raising the next round. And the list goes on. Our Venture Partners have done this at Apple, Acorn, VLSI Vision, STMicroelectronics, Texas Instruments, Bosch Sensortec, Heptagon and NIL Technology, amongst others. They also know what happens to ways of working when a small team suddenly isn't small anymore, and how to keep a company fast while it grows up.

And then there's the ending. Nearly all of us have been through an exit, as founders or as investors. So we invest with the exit in mind from the first meeting. Who buys this company one day, and what has to be true by then? We bring that experience to founders early, so they can use it to shape their company.

Alex and Graeme are both at Slush this week. Building in semis, photonics or quantum? Come say hi.
