From Chips to Chiplets: The Semiconductor Packaging Revolution

As Moore's Law slows, the semiconductor industry is shifting from monolithic chips to modular chiplet architectures. Here's why this matters for European deep tech — and where we see the biggest opportunities.

Deep Tech Semiconductors Investment Thesis

The semiconductor industry is at an inflection point. For decades, progress meant cramming more transistors onto a single piece of silicon — Moore's Law in action. But as we approach the physical limits of shrinking transistors, the industry is pivoting to a fundamentally different approach: chiplets.

Instead of building one massive monolithic chip, chiplet architectures break functionality into smaller, specialized silicon dies that are connected through advanced packaging. Think of it as moving from a single large building to a modular campus — each building optimized for its purpose, connected by high-speed pathways.

Why This Matters for European Deep Tech

Europe has historically been strong in semiconductor equipment and materials — companies like ASML, Infineon, and STMicroelectronics are global leaders. The chiplet revolution opens new investment opportunities in the packaging layer — an area where European startups are increasingly active.

Cloudberry Thesis

We believe the advanced packaging market will grow from $38B in 2024 to over $85B by 2030. European companies innovating in heterogeneous integration, interconnect technology, and testing infrastructure are uniquely positioned to capture outsized value in this shift.

The key areas we're watching closely include silicon interposer technology, hybrid bonding at sub-micron pitch, and thermal management solutions for 3D stacked architectures. These are the enabling technologies that make chiplet architectures practical at scale.
